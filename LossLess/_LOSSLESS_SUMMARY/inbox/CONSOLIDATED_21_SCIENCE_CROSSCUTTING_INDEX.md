## 1. Science Does Not Begin From Nothing

**[CLAIM -- foundational]:** Science is powerful because it limits itself.

It studies patterns that can be observed, modeled, measured, tested, repeated, corrected, and publicly compared.

But that method does not begin from absolute nothing.

Before science can operate, several conditions are already in play.

They are not usually treated as theological claims. They are the floor under scientific practice.

## 2. Science's Starting Suppositions

### S1 - There Is Something To Study

**[CLAIM -- S1]:** Science begins with reality being present enough to investigate.

It may study particles, fields, space-time, organisms, brains, behavior, stars, data, or instruments. But some object/domain/state/process must be available.

Science can model what exists inside its domain. Science does not, by its ordinary method, explain why there is something rather than nothing.

### S2 - Reality Has Distinguishable States

**[CLAIM -- S2]:** Science requires difference.

There must be distinguishable states, variables, systems, signals, events, or measurements.

No distinction, no data. No data, no science.

Distinction is not optional. It is the condition under which observation becomes informative.

### S3 - Relations Are Lawful Enough To Model

**[CLAIM -- S3]:** Science requires that distinguishable things stand in relations.

Particles interact. Fields couple. Signals transmit. Causes produce effects. Systems exchange energy, matter, or information.

If nothing related to anything else in a stable way, there would be no law, model, experiment, prediction, or explanation.

### S4 - Patterns Persist Enough To Be Rechecked

**[CLAIM -- S4]:** Science requires regularity.

Not perfect human access. Not no uncertainty. But enough stability that an observation can be compared, repeated, corrected, and refined.

If every law drifted without constraint, experiment would not accumulate knowledge.

### S5 - Measurement Is Possible

**[CLAIM -- S5]:** Science needs measurement.

Measurement requires:

- a system or event
- an instrument or observer
- an interaction
- a scale or standard
- a record
- a way to compare results

Measurement is not one object staring at another object. It is a structured relation that produces a public or recoverable result.

### S6 - Information Can Be Preserved And Transmitted

**[CLAIM -- S6]:** Science requires information.

Data must be recorded. Signals must survive long enough to be interpreted. Errors must be detectable. Models must be communicable.

The scientific community exists because information can move from one mind, instrument, paper, dataset, or experiment to another.

### S7 - Reason Can Track Reality

**[CLAIM -- S7]:** Science assumes that rational inference is not wholly detached from reality.

Hypotheses can be tested. Models can be better or worse. Contradictions matter. Evidence changes conclusions.

This does not mean scientists are infallible. It means science assumes error is corrigible because reason, observation, and reality can be brought into contact.

### S8 - Public Correction Is Possible

**[CLAIM -- S8]:** Science is not private revelation.

Its claims become stronger when they survive:

- replication
- peer challenge
- instrument improvement
- statistical correction
- adversarial review
- predictive risk

Science depends on the possibility that one observer's error can be exposed by another observer, a better instrument, or a better model.

### S9 - The Method Has Boundaries

**[CLAIM -- S9]:** Science studies repeatable, measurable, modelable patterns within reality.

It does not possess an instrument that stands outside all reality and tests the source of reality from the outside.

This is not a weakness. It is the scope of the method.

## 3. Science-Side Axiom Stack

The science lane can be compressed into operational axioms. These are not theology. They are the conditions science uses.

### Axiom SCI-A1 - Presence

**[AXIOM]:** There is some domain sufficiently present to be investigated.

### Axiom SCI-A2 - Distinction

**[AXIOM]:** The domain contains distinguishable states, events, variables, or structures.

### Axiom SCI-A3 - Relation

**[AXIOM]:** Distinguishable states can stand in lawful or statistically characterizable relation.

### Axiom SCI-A4 - Regularity

**[AXIOM]:** Some relations persist enough to support comparison, prediction, or retesting.

### Axiom SCI-A5 - Measurement

**[AXIOM]:** Interactions between systems and instruments can produce records that track some feature of the domain.

### Axiom SCI-A6 - Information

**[AXIOM]:** Records can preserve and transmit differences with enough fidelity for correction and interpretation.

### Axiom SCI-A7 - Inference

**[AXIOM]:** Reasoning over records can improve alignment between model and domain.

### Axiom SCI-A8 - Public Correction

**[AXIOM]:** Scientific claims can be challenged, replicated, revised, or rejected through shared procedures.

### Axiom SCI-A9 - Scope Boundary

**[AXIOM]:** Science's authority is strongest inside measurable, modelable, repeatable patterns and weaker when asked to adjudicate ultimate metaphysical ground.

## 4. The Science Story From The Beginning

### 4.1 Cosmology

Current science commonly begins the observable universe story with a hot, dense early state.

The Big Bang model is not an explosion into empty space. It is the expansion and cooling of space-time and its contents from an early high-energy condition.

Science can describe:

- cosmic expansion
- background radiation
- nucleosynthesis
- large-scale structure
- galaxy formation
- stellar formation
- planetary systems

Science still carries open questions:

- why these laws?
- why this initial condition?
- why a universe at all?
- what, if anything, preceded or grounded the Big Bang?
- how to unify quantum mechanics and general relativity?
- what dark matter and dark energy are?

**[CLAIM -- cosmology]:** Science's universe begins as lawful, structured, evolving, measurable, and mathematically describable.

### 4.2 Matter And Fields

Physics does not treat matter as simple solid stuff at the bottom.

Modern physics speaks in terms of:

- quantum fields

- interactions
- symmetries
- conservation laws
- forces

**[CLAIM -- matter/fields]:** The world is not explained by isolated objects alone. It is explained by relations, fields, transformations, constraints, and invariants.

### 4.3 Information

Information enters whenever distinctions are preserved, transmitted, encoded, erased, copied, measured, or corrected.

Information is not merely human meaning. It has physical cost and structure.

Examples:

- Shannon communication: source, channel, receiver
- Landauer principle: erasure has thermodynamic cost
- error correction: data, check, recovery
- measurement: system, interaction, record
- DNA: biological information encoded and copied

**[CLAIM -- information]:** Reality is not only material; it is also informationally structured.

### 4.4 Life

Life appears as organized, self-maintaining, information-processing, energy-throughput structure.

Living systems:

- maintain local order by exchanging energy and matter
- encode and transmit information
- repair damage
- adapt to environment
- reproduce
- regulate internal state
- use feedback

**[CLAIM -- life]:** Life is not a violation of thermodynamics. Life is possible because open systems can maintain local order by drawing on energy gradients.

Science has not closed the consciousness question.

It studies neural correlates, cognition, integrated information, attention, perception, memory, agency, and reportability.

But consciousness creates a boundary problem:

- the observer is also a system
- first-person experience is not identical to third-person measurement
- models of consciousness must account for subjectivity, unity, intentionality, and self-reference

**[CLAIM -- consciousness]:** Consciousness is at least the point where information, embodiment, self-reference, and reportable experience converge.

## The Threshold Between God and Physics

The Big Bang is often presented as the beginning of everything.

Strictly speaking, it is not.

It is the earliest physical regime our cosmological models attempt to describe. But before a physicist can write an equation for an expanding universe, several things must already be in place -- not earlier in clock time, because clock time is part of the universe being described, but earlier in logical order.

**[CLAIM]:** Science cannot begin with an explosion. It must begin with the conditions that make an explosion, a law, a measurement, or a history intelligible at all.

## What Must Exist Before a World

### 1. Something must exist

Before science can model anything, there must be something to model. A field, a state, a geometry, a law, or a space of possibilities must be available as the subject of the theory.

Science can describe how an existing state changes. It does not, by its ordinary methods, derive the bare fact that there is a state rather than none.

**Being: something is available to possess a state.**

| Theology says | Science says |
|---|---|
| God is. Aseity -- existence that requires nothing beneath it. | There is something to study. Science presupposes this without grounding it. |

### 2. Differences must be real

A universe containing no distinction would contain no information. There would be no difference between one state and another, between here and there, between signal and noise, between possibility and actuality.

Every measurement begins by distinguishing one result from another. Every bit requires at least two distinguishable states.

**Distinction: what exists can be this rather than that.**

| Theology says | Science says |
|---|---|
| The Father is not the Son. The persons are distinct. Distinction is constitutive, not accidental. | Every variable presupposes that its possible values are not identical. Without distinction, no information, no measurement, no physics. |

### 3. Differences must stand in relation

Distinct objects that never interact cannot form a universe. Physics does not merely inventory separate things. It studies relations -- matter related to geometry, charge related to field, system related to environment, cause related to consequence.

Even multiplication assumes relation. The expression G*K does not contain two isolated quantities. The dot means they can participate in one joint operation.

**Relation: distinguishable realities can interact in a lawful way.**

| Theology says | Science says |
|---|---|
| Perichoresis -- the persons indwell each other. Relation is not added to the Trinity; relation IS the Trinity. | Without relation, there may be plurality, but there is no physics. Every force, every field, every law describes a relation. |

### 4. Relations must be ordered

Not every imaginable transition is physically permitted. Some transformations conserve quantities. Some states are stable, others unstable. Some paths are allowed, others forbidden. Some descriptions remain invariant when the reference frame changes.

Science assumes more than interaction. It assumes lawful interaction.

**Order: relations are constrained by laws, symmetries, and invariants.**

| Theology says | Science says |
|---|---|
| Love -- the eternal movement of divine life toward the good. Orientation is not imposed on the Trinity; orientation IS the Trinity's character. | The universe is not merely relational. Its relations possess structure. Law is the grammar governing what particles and fields may do. |

## The First Four Are the Four Debts

**[CLAIM -- four debts]:** These are the four debts from Part Zero:

- Existence -> aseity
- Distinction -> persons
- Relation -> perichoresis
- Orientation -> love

Every system on earth uses all four. No system pays for them. They are assumed, used, and never grounded. The Trinity pays all four at the root. Science uses all four operationally but does not, by its own method, ground any of them.

That is not an insult to science. It is the boundary of the register.

## What Must Exist Before a History

The four debts give you a world that can have states, differences, interactions, and laws. But they do not yet give you a world that has happened. For that, you need more.

### 5. A space of possibilities must exist

**[CLAIM -- possibility]:** Before one physical history can occur, the theory must distinguish it from the histories that could have occurred but did not. Physics expresses this through state spaces, configuration spaces, phase spaces, Hilbert spaces, possible trajectories, candidate geometries.

A law without possible states governs nothing. A state without alternatives contains no selection.

**Possibility: reality admits a structured range of allowable configurations.**

### 6. Possibilities must be constrained

**[CLAIM -- constraint]:** A possibility space by itself is too large. A scientific theory requires a rule that says which possibilities are admissible, dynamically reachable, stable, probable, or extremal. This may appear as an action functional, a conservation law, a boundary condition, a symmetry, a probability measure.

Possibility supplies alternatives. Constraint gives those alternatives shape.

**Constraint: not every conceivable configuration is equally permitted.**

### 7. One possibility must become actual

**[CLAIM -- actualization, the seam]:** This is the seam.

A state space may contain many possible states. A law may assign them amplitudes, probabilities, weights. A boundary condition may narrow the field. Still, possibility is not actuality.

At some point, one history is the history. One result is recorded. One configuration becomes the physical world through which every later event proceeds.

**Actualization: a structured possibility becomes a definite physical fact.**

Science encounters this problem in several forms -- a path becomes the realized trajectory, a symmetric law enters one broken-symmetry phase, a quantum state produces a registered outcome, a field settles into one vacuum, an initial condition becomes this universe's history.
