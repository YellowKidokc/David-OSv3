# THE MORAL SUBSTRATE — Paper 4 of 7
## The Domain Audit

*David Lowe | Theophysics Research Program | POF 2828*

---

Name one science that has no concept of things going wrong.

Take your time. Search every discipline. Every field. Every department in every university on Earth.

You will not find it.

---

We have established the chain. Language is prior to math. Math is prior to matter. The child knows all three as a single structure before instruction. And the denial of this structure is self-defeating.

Now we do something simple. We open the dictionary of every scientific domain and we look for two categories of words: words that mean "working as it should" and words that mean "broken."

This is not interpretation. This is inventory.

---

**Physics.**

Stable. Unstable. Coherent. Decoherent. Ordered. Disordered. Equilibrium. Perturbation. Bound state. Decay.

The physicist does not need a theologian to tell him that a stable orbit is preferable to a decaying one. The structure tells him. A system in equilibrium persists. A system in decay collapses. The vocabulary is baked into the discipline.

**Biology.**

Healthy. Pathological. Symbiotic. Parasitic. Alive. Dead. Functional. Dysfunctional. Homeostatic. Diseased.

The biologist does not need an ethicist to tell her that a healthy cell is better than a cancerous one. The structure tells her. Health sustains. Disease consumes. The vocabulary is baked into the discipline.

**Information Theory.**

Signal. Noise. Fidelity. Corruption. Compression. Loss. Bandwidth. Interference. Coherence. Entropy.

Shannon explicitly tried to remove meaning from information theory. He said the semantic aspects of communication are irrelevant to the engineering problem. He built the most rigorously neutral framework in the history of science. And it *still* has signal and noise. It *still* has fidelity and corruption. The moral structure survived the most determined attempt to remove it.

**Thermodynamics.**

Order. Entropy. Reversible. Irreversible. Work. Waste. Efficiency. Dissipation.

The second law does not care about human morality. But it describes a universe where structure degrades, energy disperses, and useful work becomes unavailable — unless something outside the system acts to restore it. That is not a metaphor for theology. But it is the same shape.

**Economics.**

Growth. Decay. Surplus. Deficit. Productive. Extractive. Solvent. Bankrupt. Investment. Waste.

**Neuroscience.**

Integrated. Dissociated. Plastic. Rigid. Functional. Damaged. Connected. Severed.

**Computer Science.**

Valid. Corrupted. Secure. Breached. Optimized. Degraded. Running. Crashed.

**Psychology.**

Coherent. Fragmented. Resilient. Traumatized. Attached. Dissociated. Mature. Regressed.

**Medicine.**

Healing. Disease. Immunity. Infection. Regeneration. Degeneration. Vitality. Morbidity.

**Logic.**

Valid. Fallacious. Sound. Unsound. Consistent. Contradictory. Complete. Incomplete.

**Language itself.**

True. False. Meaningful. Meaningless. Clear. Obscure. Honest. Deceptive.

---

Every domain. Every field. No exceptions.

One column of terms describes things working as they should — coherent, healthy, valid, alive, ordered, productive.

The other column describes things broken — decoherent, pathological, fallacious, dead, disordered, extractive.

Call the first column whatever you want. Call it "functional." Call it "optimal." Call it "normative."

Call the second column whatever you want. Call it "dysfunctional." Call it "degraded." Call it "entropic."

You have just said "good" and "bad" in eleven different lab coats.

---

Now. Why?

If the universe were morally neutral — if "good" and "bad" were purely human projections onto an indifferent cosmos — then we would expect at least one domain to resist the pattern. At least one discipline should have a vocabulary that is genuinely flat, genuinely directionless, genuinely free of the constructive/destructive distinction.

No one has found it. Not for lack of trying. Shannon *tried*. He stripped his framework down to pure syntax. And it still has signal and noise. It still has a direction that works and a direction that doesn't. The moral structure survived the most ruthless attempt at neutrality in the history of formal systems.

This is not cultural. A society could decide to call sickness "health" and bankruptcy "growth," but the bridge would still collapse under the wrong load and the patient would still die of the untreated infection. The vocabulary is not arbitrary labeling. It tracks something real. Something structural. Something that does not care what you call it.

Every formal system humans have ever built has a constructive direction and a destructive direction. An ordered state and a disordered state. A way things *work* and a way things *break*.

And nobody had to *put* it there. Nobody designed it in. It was already there when the discipline was invented. Every science discovers it. No science installed it.

**Every domain has good and bad terms because the substrate has good and bad terms.**

**The vocabulary is not painted on. It is inherited.**

**You are not reading morality into science. You are reading morality out of it.**
