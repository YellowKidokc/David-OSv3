# Rule Brick governing principles

1. Identity comes from content and provenance, never from a filename alone.
2. Every conclusion names its evidence, counterevidence, unknowns, and engine.
3. Confidence ranks proposals; confidence never grants authority to mutate files.
4. Corroborating media, papers, codes, folders, and identifiers increase support only when their relationship is independently evidenced.
5. Exact-byte duplicates and near-duplicates are different claims. A one-word difference may represent a revision, correction, branch, or canonical distinction.
6. Paths are relationships. Renaming or moving can break code, links, sync, publishing, shortcuts, and human navigation.
7. Deletion is never inferred from similarity. Prefer leave, copy, quarantine, or archive; require explicit human approval for deletion.
8. Project roots, canonical material, secrets, regulated files, and sync conflicts receive conservative gates.
9. Every work session registers intent before work, records evidence during work, and writes an outcome receipt after work.
10. Learning records preference and outcomes but never silently changes governing principles or canonical status.

Rule Brick is advisory. David's explicit decision remains the authority for rename, move, deletion, promotion, canonization, and permanent rules.
